Authority over AI spending should be distributed across three governance tiers: operational managers approve low-cost, single-team AI tools; IT and Finance leaders jointly approve mid-range AI investments with cross-team impact; and the CIO, CFO, or an investment committee approves strategic AI platforms or multi-year commitments.

No single function should hold sole authority, because AI spending sits at the intersection of technology risk, financial exposure, and business value.

AI spending thresholds vary by organization size, but a common tiered model sets department-level authority at under $10,000 per year, IT and Finance joint approval between $10,000 and $100,000, and executive or committee review above $100,000.

OpEx spending is often approved faster and sits within operational budgets, which makes it more vulnerable to accumulating without strategic oversight.

Without that structure, AI spending becomes a shadow IT problem dressed in modern technology.
